# Indoor AR OCR and device checks

`IndoorRoomIdentityMatcher.Match` checks the selected building and floor against the directory. The live adapters use `MatchSign` to recognize doors across the mapped VEC floors without assuming the destination is the user's floor. A match identifies a directory entry; it does not prove that OCR was correct or that the phone is localized.

The Android adapter uses bundled ML Kit Latin text recognition (v2, `com.google.mlkit:text-recognition`) through `IndoorRoomOcrDemo`. It reads the AR Foundation camera's existing CPU image stream, downsizes frames to at most 1280x720, submits at most two frames per second, and drops frames while conversion or recognition is in flight. No camera session restart or image upload occurs. Recognized text goes to `IndoorRoomIdentityMatcher.MatchSign`, which accepts a VEC door on floors 1–5 from the whole text or any single line; a frame naming two rooms is `Ambiguous`. Its `IndoorRoomMatch` result contains directory identity only. The plugin also returns each word's four corner points, which feed the calibrated pose solver described below. The 1280x720 size and the corner coordinates have not run on an Android device.

For Android device validation, build and install the exported Unity AAR in the host app. Check cold startup offline to confirm the bundled model.

The iOS adapter reads AR Foundation's existing CPU image stream, downsizes frames to at most 1920x1440, and submits a frame at most every 0.3 s while idle. Recognition runs on a background queue with Google ML Kit's bundled Latin text recognizer (linked into `UnityFramework` by the export; see [iOS dependencies and linking](../../docs/ios-export.md#arcore-api-key-for-outdoor-ar)). The plugin turns the frame upright itself and returns each word's four corner points in that upright image. If ML Kit or its model bundle is unavailable, or the frame cannot be read, the adapter reports `RecognizerUnavailable`; a valid frame with no text reports `NoText`. It does not switch OCR engines. ML Kit has linked but has not run on a phone. It does not restart the camera session, upload images, log OCR text, call MultiSet, or produce a pose by itself. Device recognition has not been validated, and neither has the time or heat cost of the larger frame; the `ocrMs=` value in each `[IndoorAr] sign read` log line is the capture-to-result time to tune it from.

On both Android and iOS, scan at least one VEC room sign on each of floors 1–5. For each floor, record the returned text, match status, matched room, and matched floor. Verify the match against the sign and the floor where you scanned it. Repeat scans in portrait and landscape, in low light, and with soft focus. Check repeated scans for memory growth or rendering stalls. This repository has no device result yet.

## Sign-assisted localization

`IndoorArRunner` subscribes to both adapters when `signLocalization` is on (the default), including while pinned. Turning the flag off disables both adapters. The shipped runner uses `MultiSet` for camera pose. `SignFirst` remains available on the `IndoorArRunner` component for sign calibration experiments.

`IndoorSignTracker` confirms a room after two reads within four seconds. A frame naming two rooms resets the count; text with no room number is ignored. A confirmed sign supplies identity as described below. After a MultiSet answer without a pose, the one retry starts after three seconds or at the existing 30 s timeout, whichever comes first. If a confirmed sign is in view, it starts at once instead. The attempt budget does not change. Confirmed rereads of the same room also refresh floor evidence and feed the pose solver, so recovery does not require finding a different sign.

One sign read alone never pins the door, shows the arrow, or sets `timeToFirstLocalizationMs`; only a solved pose from a validated sign (`SignFirst`) or a MultiSet answer does.

The Flutter `google_mlkit_text_recognition` plugin was evaluated and not adopted. Flutter does not own the camera while the Unity AR activity runs, so the plugin would need a second camera session or a frame copy between the engines. Both native adapters use ML Kit's bundled Latin recognizer on the AR camera's own frames instead.

## Signs first, MultiSet last (`SignFirst`, not device-verified)

OCR evidence has two levels, and only the second one moves the map.

- **Room recognized.** A confirmed sign names a room. While scanning, the HUD shows it ("Room VEC 120 detected · Floor 1") and names the destination's floor if different. A destination sign asks the user to keep scanning to confirm the door; Slow down takes precedence. It is an identity, not a position: no XYZ is claimed. See the [host bridge](../../docs/ios-export.md#host-bridge) for result and status fields.
- **Camera pose localized.** Only `IndoorSignPose` on a sign whose calibration entry is `validated` for this phone's `SystemInfo.deviceModel` (`validatedDevice`) can pose the camera, and the runner accepts the pose only while the session is localizing or pinned, on a supported floor consistent with current sign evidence.

`SignFirstLocalizer` wraps the MultiSet localizer. With OCR enabled, the session starts scanning without waiting for MultiSet authentication; an authentication failure removes only the fallback, even when no sign is calibrated. MultiSet waits through an initial 15 s hold, including with today's empty calibration. OCR continues throughout. With OCR disabled, calibration availability has no effect: the session waits for MultiSet authentication and requests localization without the sign hold after authentication succeeds. An accepted sign pose drops queued fallback intent and quarantines any outstanding reply; an accepted MultiSet pose prevents later sign poses from moving the map until tracking loss resets the localization attempt. Terminal failure, delivery, or cancellation clears queued and deferred fallback intent even while a failure card remains visible, so late authentication cannot dispatch a request for the finished session. Outstanding SDK requests retain the lock described below.

MultiSet writes to a private proxy transform, separate from the navigation root in both hierarchy directions. Before either success callback can copy its pose to navigation, the runner validates the candidate camera height against current sign and supported-floor evidence. A rejected pose completes the SDK request, leaves navigation unchanged, and follows the normal retry path. The SDK blur check is disabled so it cannot reject or mutate a request outside this callback gate.

A process-wide lock permits at most one SDK request across indoor sessions. Requests wait for AR tracking before starting, including queued and delayed requests; session timeouts continue while tracking is unavailable. Only the SDK's actual success or failure callback releases the lock; session changes, owner destruction, and timers never do. Destroying the app localizer quarantines its reply regardless of whether its SDK manager was destroyed first. Its completion listener remains only to drain a genuine completion. OCR continues while fallback is blocked. After 30 s in flight or queued behind a live SDK request, that session's fallback becomes unavailable and logs `[IndoorAr] multiset fallback unavailable: prior request unresolved`. Waiting only for tracking does not count toward this timeout. A genuine late completion frees the lock for a current request but never applies the stale pose or restores fallback in the timed-out session. If no callback arrives, the lock stays held until process restart; there is no verified SDK cancellation. `MultiSetLocalizerTests.DestroyedOwnerKeepsGlobalLockUntilItsRealSdkCompletion` covers both destruction orders. State logs report `multisetFallback=available`, `inFlight`, `waiting`, or `unavailable`; SignFirst reports `unavailable: authentication failed` when authentication fails.

### One sign, from its corners (not device-verified)

Reading a room number says which door is near, not where the camera is. The calibrated solver uses the observed text-box corners, camera intrinsics and the sign geometry recorded in `IndoorSignCalibration.json` to estimate camera pose. The calibration ships empty, and a sign is trusted only on the device model on which it was validated. Room identity alone never poses the map.

The solver uses the captured image corners as the text-box geometry. Those corners are approximate and have not been checked against physical sign measurements.

Remaining limits:

- The camera pose is read when the image is acquired; the two clocks are not synchronised beyond that.
- None of the thresholds were tuned on a device.

The device logs sign-pose status and solve time without logging the camera pose or recognized text. Physical sign calibration and device validation are still required before claiming aligned AR.

### Room-only directions (no validated pose)

A confirmed sign sets the current-floor chip and helps reject a MultiSet pose that resolves to a different floor. Routing uses the localized camera pose on the scan-derived walk grid. Reading the destination sign on the requested floor opens the arrival card immediately. Done returns `arrived` with the existing `signRoom` field. Step-free navigation keeps its existing refusal when the route cannot be verified.

Gaps: the repo has no door-adjacency or turn graph, only the scan grid plus directory markers, so directions are distance, first passed door and connector. The Flutter bridge status does not carry the directions text. After the VPS budget expires (at most 60 s), OCR-enabled sessions keep scanning and updating room directions. Queued and deferred MultiSet intent is cleared, fallback stays disabled for that session, and any actual SDK request retains the global lock until its callback. A later validated sign pose, including a reread of the same sign, switches to precise guidance without restarting the camera. Status remains `localizing` with `localized:false` and `fallbackAvailable:false` until that pose is accepted. With OCR disabled, Unity shows the failure card. See the [Try again behavior](../../docs/floor-evidence-contract.md#what-unity-does). Unity displays the directions; the host status (`roomRecognized`, `roomFloor`, `localized`) is unchanged and carries only the room, so a host can show its own text but gets none from Unity. None of this has been checked on a device.

## Hallway route

`IndoorWalkGridExport` (menu Indoor AR > Rebuild Walk Grid From Scan) rasterizes `Assets/building scans/floor 1 + 2.glb` into `Resources/IndoorWalkGrid.json`: 0.25 m cells in the MapSet frame, walkable where the scan has a floor surface and nothing between 0.4 m and 1.4 m above it. `IndoorWalkGrid` runs A* over it from the camera to the door, and the guidance follows the route instead of pointing straight at the door: a visible floor ribbon (`IndoorRoutePath`) along the cleared stretch, or the arrow aimed at the next route point when the ribbon cannot be drawn or is outside the camera view. The HUD shows "Hallway route to" with the route length. Routes snap endpoints within 1.6 m of a walkable cell. Smoothing checks every crossed cell and blocked diagonal neighbor. Every HUD update checks clearance to a waypoint ahead, including on cached routes. If the camera is in a blocked cell, clearance is checked from the nearest walkable cell within the snap radius; if none is reachable or no forward waypoint has clearance, the arrow is hidden. `IndoorWalkGridTests` covers L-corners, blocked neighbors and the shipped VEC136 camera-position regression. If no grid route exists, no clearance can be verified, or no safe floor connector exists, the HUD shows "Route unavailable" without an arrow and preserves any non-spatial room directions.

On export, the editor warns when a directory marker cannot snap to its floor's largest walkable region; the warning does not prevent export. Reviewed doorway rectangles in `IndoorWalkGridExport.Overrides` force matching cell centres walkable. Add an override only after inspecting the mesh or walking through the opening; see the list for its current entries and constraints. `IndoorWalkGridTests` checks the gate and doorway override.

The runner caches both supported floor grids per session for routing and pose validation, refreshing them on a new session. The HUD uses the current sign floor while the sign remains authoritative. See the [floor evidence contract](../../docs/floor-evidence-contract.md#what-unity-does) for when Unity drops a floor veto. Without current sign evidence, camera height must be 1–2.2 m above exactly one supported floor elevation; otherwise the HUD reports an unknown floor and shows no arrow. Candidate sign and MultiSet poses must satisfy this height gate and agree with active floor evidence.

On another supported floor, ordinary routing selects the shortest reachable grid route to a directory stairs or elevator marker on the user's floor; the HUD names the connector and destination floor. This is not a verified path through the connector. A navigation request with `"stepFree": true` shows "A step-free route to this destination cannot be confirmed" and returns `unavailable` with `STEP_FREE_UNVERIFIED`, because neither same-floor grid segments nor connectors have verified accessibility attributes. Localize-only requests do not request a route. Omitting the field preserves ordinary routing. The Flutter host still needs to serialize its saved mobility preference in `IndoorArRequest.toJson`.

Coverage today: all 9 mapped floor-1 doors and all 8 floor-2 doors snap onto the grid and connect to each other (`IndoorWalkGridTests`). The scan has hallways only, not room interiors, and no floor surface under the VEC119, 120, 121 and 124 markers, so their routes end at the nearest hallway cell, up to 1.6 m from the marker. Grid paths do not join the floors, and there is no drawn path line. The route has not been walked on a device. Re-run the export when the scan or the MapSet alignment changes.

## Precise pose from a sign (inert until surveyed and validated)

`IndoorSignPose` solves the camera pose in the MapSet frame from the four image corners of one calibrated sign, with a 3 px reprojection gate and two checks. The camera must be in front of the sign and 0.3 to 5 m from it (`Implausible` otherwise), and no other candidate pose may also reproject within 3 px while moving the camera more than 5 degrees in yaw or 0.25 m in plan (`Ambiguous` otherwise). `SignFirst` uses it; on acceptance the navigation origin is moved (yaw only) so the AR camera sits at the solved pose, without needing a MultiSet result. Any already outstanding SDK request remains isolated until its terminal callback.

On iOS and Android the solve is OpenCV 4.14.0 `solvePnPGeneric` with `SOLVEPNP_IPPE`, each of its two solutions refined by `solvePnPRefineLM` (`tools/sign-pnp/IndoorSignPnp.cpp`). The pose is the better-fitting refinement; the two raw solutions and the other refinement are the candidates for the ambiguity check. Both are needed: near a frontal view raw IPPE can return a pose centimetres off that still passes the gate, which refinement corrects, and far away refinement can pull both flips into one pose, which only the raw pair shows. Raw IPPE also fails outright on some exactly symmetric views; those are rejected by the gate, not solved. The Editor and EditMode tests have no OpenCV build and use a managed planar homography plus its mirrored candidate instead, so survey validation (`IndoorSignPose.Validate`, run by the importer in the Editor) checks the managed solve, not the phone's. shortcut: build OpenCV for the Editor too if real captures show the two disagree. There is no lens distortion model, the thresholds are untuned, and OCR gives text-box rectangles rather than perspective corners, which limits accuracy at oblique views.

Native solver binaries are not tracked; the source is `tools/sign-pnp/IndoorSignPnp.cpp`. Before an iOS or Android build, run `tools/build-sign-pnp.sh ios` (needs Xcode) or `tools/build-sign-pnp.sh android` (needs the editor's Android Build Support NDK); `tools/build-indoor-ar-aar.sh` runs the Android one itself. The first run downloads the official OpenCV release zips from GitHub and checks them against the sha256 values pinned in the script, then caches them in `.cache/opencv` (`OPENCV_CACHE` overrides), so later runs work offline. Offline with no cached zip, or with a zip that fails the check, it stops with an error and uses nothing. Outputs (`Assets/Plugins/iOS/libindoorsignpnp.a`, `Assets/Plugins/Android/libs/arm64-v8a/libindoorsignpnp.so`), staged notice resources, and the cache are gitignored. Both exports require `Assets/StreamingAssets/ThirdPartyNotices/OpenCV/`, containing the OpenCV license, project notice, and bundled SDK third-party license files; Unity packages this folder into iOS and Android runtime assets. The Android export path is `unityLibrary/src/main/assets/ThirdPartyNotices/OpenCV/`.

The math is covered by synthetic round-trip tests, and the phone path by fixtures of the native solver's output (`tools/sign-pnp/ippe-fixtures.py` prints them, with `opencv-python-headless==4.14.0.94`). The native library was linked and run on a Mac only, not on a phone. Nothing here has run on a device, and no pose accuracy is claimed.

A sign poses the camera only when its entry in `Assets/IndoorAr/Resources/IndoorSignCalibration.json` (shipped as `{"signs": []}`) is `validated` and its `validatedDevice` equals the phone's `SystemInfo.deviceModel`; otherwise the status is `NoCalibration`, `Unvalidated`, or `OtherDevice`. Entries are produced only by the survey importer, never by hand or from a MultiSet estimate:

1. On site, per sign: measure `widthM` and `heightM` of the digits' box the OCR corners outline (not the sign plate), `centerInMap` of that box in the MapSet frame, and `intoWallHeadingDeg` (the door-marker convention below).
2. From a development build on the phone to validate, stand at surveyed camera positions in front of the sign. Each frame whose status is `NoCalibration`, `Unvalidated`, or `OtherDevice` logs `[IndoorAr] sign capture room=... fx= fy= cx= cy= corners=... device=...`: image intrinsics and corner pixels only, no position and no text. Release builds never print it.
3. Write the survey JSON: `signs` holds the measured entries (`doorKey`, `widthM`, `heightM`, `centerInMap`, `intoWallHeadingDeg`), and `samples` holds one object per capture with the surveyed `cameraInMap`, the surveyed `headingDeg`, and the `capture` log line verbatim.
4. Run **Indoor AR > Import Sign Survey** in the editor. It writes the calibration and logs `<doorKey>: samples=N validated=True|False` per sign. A sign is validated, for the captures' device model, only if every capture in the survey parses, the sign has at least 3 captures, all from one device model, and every one of them solves to within 0.3 m and 5 degrees of its surveyed pose. Anything the survey file claims about validation is ignored.

The device log prints `[IndoorAr] sign pose room= status= solveUs=` when the status changes, never the pose. See [baseline fields](BASELINE.md) for session latency metrics.

## Room identity and door poses (reuse, no second database)

`Assets/IndoorNavigation/Data/vec_floors12345.json` is the single directory. It holds the doors and other markers for floors 1–5. `IndoorMapExporter` exports them relative to `IndoorMapOrigin`. The walk grid aligns the floor 3 scan using its placement in `SampleScene` and the same `IndoorMapFrame` conversion as its room markers. `IndoorRoomIdentityMatcher` already turns sign text into a room ID from it; each `IndoorRoomLandmark` now also carries `DoorPositionInMap` and `DoorHeadingInMapDeg`.

The JSON frame is not the MapSet frame, but it differs by one rigid transform: yaw 5.356 degrees, offset (0.279, -2.212, 0.838) m (`IndoorMapFrame`). That was fitted on the 17 door rows copied from `vec_destination_points` (`IndoorArDoorMeshTests`) and holds to 0.0001 m, with the heading in the real VEC119 request (185.36) matching. `IndoorMapFrameTests` fails if the JSON or the transform drifts; re-derive the transform when the MapSet is re-aligned. The backend table stays authoritative for doors; the transform only reaches rooms or markers the request does not carry.

What a point is: a door marker, a 0.3 m cube placed by hand on the floor at the door threshold (y is within 0.5 m of the scanned floor), not a room centre, a sign, or a navigation waypoint. Its heading points from the hallway into the room (doors on opposite sides of a hallway differ by 180 degrees), which is the `intoWallHeadingDeg` convention `IndoorSignPose` uses. These two facts are read from the data, not measured.

Investigated for 068: the VEC scan and directory hold hallway geometry and door-marker positions only, so no sign calibration can be derived from them; nothing was estimated. Still unmeasured for a pose, so `IndoorSignCalibration.json` stays empty: sign width and height, the sign centre's height above the floor and its sideways offset from the door marker (signs often sit beside the door), the OCR corners, the camera intrinsics and screen-to-camera transform, and a paired comparison against `CameraInMap`. Nothing named "BlueTight" exists in this repo, the backend, or the Flutter app.
